Wind at Race Rocks and oil spill response

July 2026. Commissioned study for Garry Fletcher, Race Rocks Ecological Reserve.

Data analysis·Oil spill response

One question: how often is it too windy at Race Rocks to contain an oil spill? Containment boom and skimmers stop working at around 28 km/h, when oil is driven over and under the boom and crews cannot safely tend it. Race Rocks sits at the eastern entrance to the Strait of Juan de Fuca, in the tanker lane.

I analyzed every hourly observation from Environment and Climate Change Canada's Race Rocks station for 2021 to 2025 in Python, confirmed it was the only station there with hourly records, and left missing hours out instead of counting them as calm.

Over five years the wind was at or above the threshold 43.1% of the time. The surprise was the season. The worst months are not the winter storms but the summer, when a westerly funnels up the Strait almost every afternoon. July averaged 79.1%, during peak vessel traffic, seal haul-outs and seabird breeding. On half of all days there was no 12-hour window calm enough to work in.

These figures are conservative: they count sustained wind rather than gusts, and ignore the sea state that lingers after the wind drops. Moving the threshold anywhere from 25 to 35 km/h still leaves between 30% and 49% of hours unworkable.

Things I have built

Bowcast

Live · App Store · Source · Since June 2026. Website and API; iPhone app since September 2026.

Atmospheric physics·Software

A worldwide rainbow forecast. It works out where the sun is from your coordinates and the time, then checks whether rain is falling in the part of the sky where a rainbow would have to appear.

The number it shows is a weighted probability estimated from about 40 weather model runs, not a score I invented. When that ensemble is unavailable it says so rather than filling in a worse number in the same place.

Black Hole Census

Live · Source · Since July 2026. 3D field guide and logarithmic census.

Astronomy·Data engineering

Black hole populations are split across incompatible archives. Merger detections live in GWOSC, catalog cross-matches in VizieR, identifiers in SIMBAD, and extragalactic objects in NED. The Census brings those records into one field guide without flattening their different kinds of evidence.

Every measurement keeps its uncertainty and source. The interface shows how invisible objects are inferred through stellar motion, X-rays, the orbits of stars and gas around galactic nuclei, horizon-scale imaging, and gravitational waves.

Each record also declares how directly its mass was obtained, so a quasar mass estimated from a calibrated relation can never sit unmarked beside a stellar orbit. A separate sandbox takes any mass at all, from a person to fifty billion suns, and draws the horizon it implies next to something familiar.
